# Workload

Per-frame traces (csv: `frame_id,frame_type,size_bytes,pts_ms`) that the
streamer sends, one QUIC stream per frame. All are encoded the same way by
`make_frame_trace.sh`: x264, zerolatency, no B-frames, an I-frame every 2 s,
constant quality (the CRF in the table) capped at 3 Mbit/s, 1280x720.

| trace | source | CRF | frames | rate | I-frame median | P-frame median |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `copyrightx_lecture_crf22.csv` (main) | real lecture, 60 s clip | 22 | 1441 at 24 fps | 1.57 Mbit/s | 55.3 KB | 8.0 KB |
| `copyrightx_lecture.csv` | same clip | 20 | 1441 at 24 fps | 2.13 Mbit/s | 73.1 KB | 10.9 KB |
| `synthetic_lecture.csv` | generated by `synth_lecture.sh` | 20 | 1800 at 30 fps | 1.56 Mbit/s | 38.5 KB | 6.1 KB |

Why CRF 22 for the main results: at CRF 20 the real lecture needs 2.13 Mbit/s,
about 90% of the low_bw scenario's two paths together once QUIC overhead is
counted; queues then grow without bound and every scheduler missed about 65%
of frames (`results/copyrightx_lecture_crf20_overload/`). A live platform
would lower the bitrate on such a connection, so the evaluation uses CRF 22
(1.57 Mbit/s, the same load as the synthetic lecture): more than path A alone
carries in low_bw, so the lecture still needs both paths. The CRF 20 version
is kept because the predictor's training runs used it.

## Real lecture

William Fisher, "CopyrightX: Lecture 3.2, The Subject Matter of Copyright:
Drama and choreography", Berkman Center for Internet & Society, Harvard
University. CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:William_Fisher,_CopyrightX-_Lecture_3.2,_The_Subject_Matter_of_Copyright-_Drama_and_choreography.webm

We use the clip from 120 s to 180 s (a wide shot of the speaker, a cut to a
close-up at 132 s, a cut to a photo slide at 157 s), re-encoded as above. The
video itself is not in the repository; to rebuild the trace:

```bash
mkdir -p workload/src/copyrightx_3_2 && curl -L -o workload/src/copyrightx_3_2/lecture.webm 'https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/63/William_Fisher%2C_CopyrightX-_Lecture_3.2%2C_The_Subject_Matter_of_Copyright-_Drama_and_choreography.webm'
```

```bash
START=120 DURATION=60 FPS=24 workload/make_frame_trace.sh workload/src/copyrightx_3_2/lecture.webm copyrightx_lecture_crf22 22
```

(and with `copyrightx_lecture 20` for the CRF 20 version).

The source runs at 23.976 fps; encoding at 24 fps keeps one encoded frame
per source frame (converting to 30 fps would add duplicate frames that
encode to almost nothing).

## Synthetic lecture

Slides whose bullets appear every 3 s and change every 15 s, a moving pointer
and a small webcam inset with sensor noise, from fixed seeds (the same bytes on
every run of the script). Used for development and the first results.

```bash
workload/synth_lecture.sh && workload/make_frame_trace.sh workload/src/synthetic_lecture.mkv synthetic_lecture
```
