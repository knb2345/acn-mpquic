2 · Related work: what exists today

Multipath protocols split the video packet by packet

Multipath QUIC can already use two links in one connection. A scheduler picks a link for every packet: the faster one (MinRTT), turn by turn (round-robin), both (redundant), or by expected finish (ECF).

Commercial bonding boxes (LiveU) are closed; research that sends whole frames (MoMQ, 2026) works at a relay, not at the teacher’s phone.

The weak spot

The scheduler does not know where a frame begins and ends. It can keep one link’s buffer full while the other has room, or make a frame wait for its last packet on the slower link. Either way the frame is late.

ECF (Lim et al., CoNEXT 2017) · BLEST (IFIP Networking 2016) · DEMS (Guo et al., MobiCom 2017) · Multipath QUIC (De Coninck and Bonaventure, CoNEXT 2017; IETF draft) · XLINK (SIGCOMM 2021) · Converge (SIGCOMM 2023) · MoMQ (2026) · COMPACT (MMSys 2025) · Media over QUIC

Existing schedulers were designed for bulk data, where only total throughput matters. For live video a frame is useful only when complete and on time. If one frame's packets are spread over a 30 ms link and a 150 ms link, the frame waits for the 150 ms link. ECF (Lim et al., CoNEXT 2017) estimates completion time, but per packet, and does not know where frames begin and end. We implemented ECF ourselves as the strongest baseline.
