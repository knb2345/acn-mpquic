10 · What we learned

Frames, queues and a strong simple baseline

1. Place frames, not packets

Knowing where a frame starts and ends lets the sender split it so both parts arrive together. At the target bitrate that cut freezing by 7 to 10 points against MinRTT and ECF; placing whole frames was clearly worse on steady links. At low bitrates our version still stalls.

2. Watch the queues

MinRTT and ECF kept about 130 ms of video waiting in the fast link’s buffer. A frame scheduler has to count the bytes already waiting on each link, including those in the network’s buffer; the smoothed RTT reacts too slowly. With the true link rates (R2) F2 would freeze 2 to 3 points less.

3. Compare with round-robin

On steady links plain round-robin beat MinRTT and ECF by 3 to 14 points of frames on time, and under outages it was the best scheduler we tried. Any new scheduler should be measured against it.

Code and data

{RUNS} paired runs with raw per-run data, scripts and analysis on GitHub (knb2345/acn-mpquic). Tests: 565 in TQUIC, 12 in the streamer, 15 in the analysis.

Three lessons. The second one is also a warning: a frame scheduler is only as good as its picture of what is already queued on each link.
