9 · Our five predictions

What held and what did not

Where the bytes went at 2.6 Mbit/s. On the bandwidth-asymmetric pair F2 put 66% of the bytes on the 2.0 Mbit/s link (its share of the capacity is 62.5%), with 0.6% extra bytes. Under outages the rescue adds 6.4%.

H1 · one link is often not enough

Not tested: we did not record phone traces.

H2 · packet schedulers waste capacity

Supported. On the 30/150 ms pair MinRTT and ECF stay smooth up to 1.0 Mbit/s, an ideal link up to 2.6: 38% of the ideal, below our 80% bar (P1, P2).

H3 · frame splitting freezes less

Partly. Yes against MinRTT and ECF on the asymmetric pairs (P3, P4). On symmetric links we expected a tie, but F2 was 10 points better. Under outages it is no better.

H4 · two plain connections work as well

Not as stated. At 2.6 Mbit/s two connections did as well as one (3.6% vs 4.4% frozen), but at low bitrates our single-connection F2 stalls, so A1 stays usable up to a higher bitrate (1.0 vs 0.5 Mbit/s, P5).

H5 · bigger gain with keyframes

Not tested: we ran only the intra-refresh encoding.

Each prediction was written down with a test before any run. The primary comparisons P1 to P5 are Holm-corrected as one family.
