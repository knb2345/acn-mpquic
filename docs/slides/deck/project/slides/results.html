Time frozen, F2 vs the better of MinRTT and ECF: RTT-asymmetric 4.4% vs 14.9%, bandwidth-asymmetric 3.8% vs 10.8%, F2 lower in 8 of 8 pairs each (pre-registered P3, P4; Holm p = 0.023).

R1 (one link with the combined rate) is not shown for outages: there its single link goes dark with the outage schedule.

Close to the ideal link

On the three steady scenarios F2 is within 0.1 to 3.2 points of the ideal link’s on-time rate. Sending whole frames without splitting (F1) freezes 2 to 6 times as long (7.0% vs 1.2%, 11.2% vs 3.8%, 8.2% vs 4.4%).

Round-robin is a close rival

It ties F2 on symmetric links and does better on the RTT-asymmetric pair (2.1% vs 4.4% frozen), but worse when the rates differ (7.4% vs 3.8%).

Outages are still hard

With one link off, 1.8 Mbit/s is left for a 2.6 Mbit/s video, so 0.8 Mbit/s piles up every second. Every scheduler freezes 25% of the time or more; F2 (37.7%) is no better than MinRTT (34.3%); round-robin does best (25.1%).

All numbers come from the 8 paired runs per cell; the table is generated from analysis/tables/main_2600.csv. Against MinRTT and ECF, F2 removes most of the freezing on the steady scenarios. It is not better than round-robin everywhere: round-robin wins on the 30/150 ms pair, F2 wins when the link rates differ. Outages remain unsolved: the one-timeout rescue does not help enough.
